Before A council press release · 165 words

The Council today approved its “Homes for Brookfield” plan. Over the next five years the Council will build 1,200 new homes on former industrial land near the river. At least 35% will be “affordable housing”, meaning rent set at no more than 80% of local market rent. The plan will be paid for by a £40 million loan from the Public Works Loan Board and by selling two council-owned car parks.

After · the front page it makes

In one sentence

Brookfield will build 1,200 new homes by the river, but people disagree on whether it is a good idea.

What it says

  • The Council approved the "Homes for Brookfield" plan: 1,200 new homes on former industrial land over five years.
  • At least 35% will be "affordable housing".
  • A £40 million loan and the sale of two council car parks will pay for it.
  • Buildings of up to eight storeys will be allowed by the river, up from four.

Claims worth checking

  • 1,200 homes in five yearsCheck the council's published plan and budget for the building timetable.
  • The riverside land is at risk of floodingOpposition councillors say this. Check the official flood maps for the area.

Who it affects

  • Young families: the Council Leader says they will be able to stay in the town.
  • Local shops: opposition councillors say selling the car parks will hurt them.
  • People near the river, who may see buildings up to eight storeys tall.

Words explained

  • Affordable housing: rent set at no more than 80% of local market rent.
  • Public Works Loan Board: where the £40 million loan comes from.

Questions to ask

  • Which two car parks will be sold?
  • What will be done about the flood risk?
  • How can people take part in the consultation before March?

How it works

  1. Your textPasted, a sample, or read from a photo, a speech video or a YouTube link. You check it first.
  2. Privacy guardOn your device, it looks for emails, phone numbers, home addresses, postcodes, bank, card and National Insurance numbers, and your own name. If it finds any, the text stays on your device.
  3. Your deviceGemini Nano, built into Chrome. Private, works offline, nothing is sent. Used first whenever it can run.
    The cloudGemini through Firebase AI Logic, only when the device can't: another browser, a long text, a video, or if the device fails partway. Never with personal details.
  4. The explainerA sticker says where it ran and how long it took.

Always on your device: the privacy guard, Spot the spin, the reading age and the share picture. Check this claim sends only that one sentence. Photos are read on your device first; videos and recordings need the cloud, so the page asks before sending a file.

Plain Politics explains; it doesn't take sides. AI can make mistakes, so check important claims against the original source.
